Software Engineering Case Studies

End-to-end scenarios for applying requirements engineering, modelling, architecture, testing, quality assurance, risk management, maintenance and project planning. Each case study includes stakeholders, scope, requirements, models, acceptance criteria and engineering risks.

Case Study 1

University Campus Management System

Problem: Student records, attendance, course registration, examination schedules and results are maintained in disconnected workflows, causing duplication and delayed reporting.

Stakeholders

Students, faculty, department coordinators, examination cell, accounts office, administrators and IT support.

Core requirements

  • Role-based access for students, faculty, administrators and examination staff.
  • Course registration with prerequisites, capacity checks and approval rules.
  • Attendance entry, correction history and attendance summaries.
  • Exam scheduling, controlled marks entry, moderation and result publication.
  • Audit logs, backups, privacy controls and accessible student-facing pages.

Key models and artifacts

Context diagram, use-case diagram, domain/class model, ER diagram, activity flows for registration and result publication, sequence diagram for login and registration, requirements traceability matrix, test plan and deployment plan.

Student request→Rule validation→Approval / update→Audit & notification

Acceptance criteria

Users see only permitted records; duplicate registrations are rejected; approved changes are auditable; reports reconcile to source records; recovery procedures are tested.

Risks and controls

Incorrect result publication is mitigated by dual approval and audit trails. Privacy risk is reduced with least-privilege access, encryption and retention rules. Peak registration demand requires load testing and capacity planning.

Case Study 2

Hospital Appointment & Patient Portal

Problem: Patients need a reliable way to find clinicians, request appointments, receive reminders and view selected health information without exposing sensitive records.

Stakeholders

Patients, clinicians, reception staff, billing team, hospital administrators and security/compliance personnel.

Core requirements

  • Search clinicians by specialty, location and availability.
  • Prevent double booking and manage cancellation, rescheduling and waitlists.
  • Provide reminders and a clear appointment status history.
  • Restrict clinical information by role and patient authorization.
  • Record access events and support incident investigation.

Key models and artifacts

Use-case model, appointment state machine, sequence diagram for booking, data model, privacy threat model, interface prototypes, test scenarios and operational support plan.

Find slot→Check availability→Confirm booking→Notify patient

Acceptance criteria

Two requests cannot reserve the same clinician slot; users can see booking status; unauthorized access is denied and logged; reminders are sent according to consent and notification preferences.

Risks and controls

Protect confidentiality, availability and integrity. Use privacy-by-design, access reviews, secure recovery, clear consent and carefully tested integrations. Clinical workflows require domain-owner validation.

Case Study 3

Online Bookstore & Order Fulfilment

Problem: Customers need to discover books, manage a cart, pay securely and track orders while inventory remains consistent across concurrent purchases.

Stakeholders

Customers, catalog managers, warehouse staff, payment provider, delivery partners, customer support and finance.

Core requirements

  • Search and filter books by title, author, category, price and availability.
  • Maintain cart, shipping details, order status and order history.
  • Confirm payment status safely and avoid duplicate charges or duplicate orders.
  • Reserve or decrement stock consistently and handle failed payments.
  • Support refunds, cancellations, accessibility and customer support workflows.

Key models and artifacts

Use-case diagram, domain model for Book/Cart/Order/Payment/Shipment, order state machine, sequence diagram for checkout, component architecture, test cases and failure-mode analysis.

Browse→Cart→Payment→Fulfilment

Acceptance criteria

Out-of-stock items cannot be purchased; retries do not create duplicate orders; failed payment does not mark an order paid; order history reflects verified status transitions.

Risks and controls

Use idempotent payment handling, transaction boundaries, stock reconciliation, monitoring and tested refund procedures. Performance testing should cover sales spikes and slow external providers.

Case Study 4

Library Management System

Problem: A library needs accurate cataloguing, lending, returns, renewals, reservations, overdue notices and inventory reporting.

Stakeholders

Members, librarians, catalog administrators, finance staff and institutional management.

Core requirements

  • Search catalog records and distinguish titles from physical copies.
  • Issue and return copies while enforcing loan limits and due dates.
  • Handle reservations, renewals, overdue notices and fine policies.
  • Track lost/damaged copies and maintain a history of circulation actions.
  • Produce inventory, usage and overdue reports.

Key models and artifacts

Use-case diagram, ER model for Member/Title/Copy/Loan/Reservation, activity diagram for issue/return, business rule catalogue, test plan and migration checklist for existing records.

Search title→Check copy→Issue / reserve→Return & update

Acceptance criteria

A physical copy cannot have two active loans; due dates follow policy; returns update availability; authorized staff can trace corrections; reports match circulation records.

Risks and controls

Prevent data loss through backup and restore testing. Validate imported records, define exception handling for damaged/lost items and keep policies configurable rather than embedding assumptions in workflows.

Case Study 5

Food Delivery & Live Order Tracking

Problem: Customers, restaurants and delivery partners need coordinated order status, availability, payment and delivery updates.

Stakeholders

Customers, restaurant staff, delivery partners, operations teams, payment provider and customer support.

Core requirements

  • Display restaurant menus, availability, prices and estimated delivery times.
  • Coordinate order acceptance, preparation, pickup and delivery statuses.
  • Handle substitutions, cancellation rules, payment failures and refunds.
  • Provide location sharing with clear permissions and retention limits.
  • Notify users of meaningful status changes and record support events.

Key models and artifacts

System context, order state machine, sequence diagram across customer/restaurant/payment/delivery services, component diagram, service-level objectives, test strategy and incident response plan.

Place order→Restaurant accepts→Pickup→Delivered

Acceptance criteria

Status changes are ordered and traceable; cancelled orders trigger the correct payment outcome; unavailable items are handled transparently; tracking is shown only with permission.

Risks and controls

External services may fail or respond slowly. Use retries with limits, clear degraded states, observability, privacy safeguards and manual support paths for exceptional orders.

Case Study 6

Online Examination & Result Processing

Problem: An institution needs to schedule assessments, authorize candidates, record submissions, apply evaluation rules and publish results accurately.

Stakeholders

Students, invigilators, exam coordinators, evaluators, administrators and academic leadership.

Core requirements

  • Publish exam schedules and provide authenticated candidate access.
  • Enforce time windows, submission rules and documented accommodations.
  • Separate evaluator, moderator and result-publisher permissions.
  • Retain submission receipts, marking history, moderation and appeal records.
  • Support high concurrency, recovery, accessibility and audit reporting.

Key models and artifacts

Use-case diagram, activity flow for submission, state machine for result lifecycle, data-flow diagram, threat model, traceability matrix, load-test plan and disaster-recovery checklist.

Authenticate→Attempt→Evaluate→Moderate & publish

Acceptance criteria

Submission receipts are reliable; unauthorized result changes are blocked; publication requires the approved workflow; audit records identify who changed what and when.

Risks and controls

Plan for peak loads, network interruption, identity assurance, data integrity and appeals. Run recovery exercises and communicate incident procedures before examinations.

The Future of Software Engineering

Software engineering increasingly combines strong fundamentals with automation, security, distributed systems, human-centered design and responsible AI assistance.

AI-assisted development

AI can help generate tests, explain code and draft documentation; engineers must verify behavior, security, privacy and licensing.

Platform engineering

Reusable developer platforms and automated environments reduce friction while improving consistency and governance.

Secure by design

Threat modelling, dependency controls, identity, least privilege and continuous security checks are becoming standard practice.

Cloud-native systems

Services need resilience, observability, cost awareness, automated deployment and well-defined operational ownership.

Quality engineering

Automated checks, contract testing, continuous verification and risk-based testing bring quality into the delivery pipeline.

Sustainable software

Resource-aware algorithms and architectures measure and reduce avoidable compute, storage and energy consumption.

Industry & Careers

Software engineering practices are used across product companies, IT services, financial services, healthcare, education, government and startups. Roles include software engineer, QA engineer, business analyst, SRE, DevOps engineer, architect, product engineer and project manager.

Product companies

Focus on product discovery, architecture, experimentation, reliability, user experience and continuous delivery.

IT services & consulting

Focus on requirements, client communication, delivery governance, integration, quality assurance and maintenance.

Fintech, healthcare & enterprise

Emphasize security, compliance, auditability, reliability, privacy and controlled change management.

Startup teams

Balance fast feedback and MVP delivery with sensible architecture, automated tests and manageable technical debt.
